Methodological note. This tool applies the portfolio basis transfer — a tail-regime quantile map onto the selected target regime, then size scaling and concentration scaling, all from the fitted pooling model — to re-express historical market reserve movements on the user’s target portfolio basis. It is an upstream adjustment to the distributional input, not a tail-fitting or capital-setting method: the tail-regime step applies the model’s two fitted Student-t indices, and the actuary’s subsequent choice of distribution, fitting approach, and capital measure remains a separate modelling step.

How to Read This Tool

Purpose

This tool accompanies the paper Portfolio-Aware Scenario Transfer for Prior-Year Reserve Movements: Evidence from Lloyd’s Syndicates. It lets the reader apply the Section 4 portfolio basis transfer to an arbitrary target syndicate profile and inspect the resulting distributional shift.

What the tool does

For each historical donor syndicate-year in the eligible pool, the tool performs two sequential adjustments to the donor’s observed signed prior-year development (PYD) ratio:

The donor’s severity ratio is transferred to the target profile by the shape-aware operator Sadj = σ(Rt,Ht)·F−1νt(Fνs(Sraw/σ(Rdonor,Hdonor))), where σ(R,H) = √(σundiv² + σdiv²·[(R/Rref)(1/H)γ]2(k−1)). The dispersion σ carries an undiversifiable floor σundiv (the dispersion a very large, diversified book cannot shed) plus a diversifiable term governed by the pooling exponent k and the effective line count neff = 1/H. Fν is the Student-t CDF: each donor is standardised in its own tail regime — νs = νRITC when the donor is a reinsurance-to-close (RITC) year, νclean otherwise — and quantile-mapped onto the selected target regime νt: clean (the default; for RITC donors this is “de-RITC-ing”), RITC-affected, or a diagnostic that preserves each donor’s own regime. Whenever νs = νt the map is the identity and the operator reduces to the pure rescale λ = σ(target)/σ(donor). There is no line-level projection: the operator is the fitted dispersion model applied.

    The result is a pool of Sadj values that represent what the market’s historical PYD experience would have looked like had every donor been writing the target portfolio.

    What the tool does not do

    The adjusted distribution is an input to subsequent tail modelling, not a finished capital number. Choosing the distributional form, fitting method, and risk measure (e.g. VaR, TVaR) is the actuary’s responsibility and is outside the scope of this tool.

    Interpreting the tabs

    • Distributions — KDE density of raw market vs target-basis signed PYD ratio, plus an empirical survivor function on the adverse (positive) tail. Compressing or spreading of the adjusted curve relative to the raw curve indicates the net effect of the basis transfer.
    • Statistics — Summary statistics (n, mean, standard deviation, Q75, VaR99, VaR99.5) for the raw pool, three intermediate coalitions (tail regime only, tail + size, tail + concentration), and the fully adjusted distribution, with raw-to-target deltas.
    • Waterfall — Three-player Shapley decomposition of the raw-to-target VaR99.5 movement into tail-regime, reserve-size and concentration components. All eight coalitions of the three factors (2³ pools) are evaluated and each player’s marginal contribution is averaged over orderings, so the attribution is path-independent and the three effects sum exactly to target − raw. Under the preserve-donor-regime diagnostic the tail-regime bar is exactly zero.
    • Donor Detail — Full donor table sorted by Sadj. Click any row to see the complete worked example: LoB-level projection table, Vsize calculation, and percentage changes at each step.
    • Data & Settings — Run provenance, donor eligibility rules, size-model coefficients, and formula version.

    Donor eligibility

    The donor pool matches the paper’s vignette sample: syndicate-years with LoB-level severity reconstruction, opening reserves ≥ £5m, a non-missing signed PYD ratio, and a RELIABLE or INCOMPLETE quality tag. This is the same pool used in the Appendix A worked vignettes.
